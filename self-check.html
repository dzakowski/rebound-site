The 8 signs of rebound congestion

The self-check above asks about these eight signs. The more that apply, the more likely your congestion is being driven by the spray itself (rhinitis medicamentosa) rather than by the cold or allergy you started treating.

  1. You have used a decongestant spray for more than 3–5 days in a row. US labels set the limit at 3 days.
  2. The spray wears off sooner, so you are dosing more often than the label says.
  3. You cannot fall asleep without it.
  4. Congestion returns within hours of a dose wearing off, often worse than before you started.
  5. You carry a bottle everywhere: bag, car, desk, bedside.
  6. You feel panic or dread when the bottle is empty or missing, and make special trips to replace it.
  7. Your nose is now worse than the original cold or allergies, and only the spray helps.
  8. You have tried to stop and could not.

Doctors diagnose rebound congestion mainly from this history, sometimes with a look inside the nose. There is no blood test for it. If several of these apply to you, the quit playbook compares three ways to stop. If you have used a spray for years, read when to see an ENT first.
